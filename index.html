      Why revenue alone is a vanity metric

      Where a single order actually goes.

      Four things come out of every order you take. Three of them belong to somebody else. The fourth is the only one that pays you, and it is the one most brands never put a number against.

      • Cost of goods — what the product cost to make and put in a box.
      • Shipping — what it cost to get there, less whatever the customer covered.
      • Ad cost per order — what you paid to find that customer. The one you can move.
      • What you keep — contribution margin. The only number that pays you.

      The method

      The Compounding Loop.

      Four stages that feed each other. Most brands run one of them well and wonder why growth stalls at the same ceiling every quarter. The loop only pays when it closes — every stage hands the next one something it could not have produced alone.

      What we actually do

      01

      The Audit

      We get read-only access to your Meta account, your store and your email platform, and we spend a week inside them. You get back a written teardown: what your true cost per customer is once returns are counted, which campaigns are cannibalizing each other, where the pixel is lying to you, and the three changes that move the most money the fastest.

      Month-to-month operation of Instagram and Facebook. We write, direct and edit the creative, run the testing schedule, hold the account structure, and report on contribution margin rather than on impressions. You approve concepts. We handle everything after that.

      Before you book

      The questions people actually ask.

      What does it cost?

      We quote on the first call, once we have seen what we would be walking into. There is no price list here because the work is not the same job from one brand to the next, and a number published without knowing your account would be a number we had to walk back. Ask us on the call and you will get a straight answer before you hang up.

      Am I locked into a contract?

      Thirty days' notice, either direction, after the first ninety. We ask for ninety because the loop needs three full cycles before the compounding shows up in the numbers, and anyone promising you a transformation inside a month is selling you a spike you will pay for later.

      Why two freelancers instead of a real agency?

      Because at your stage the agency would put you with whoever they hired most recently. The senior person you met in the pitch is not the person who opens your account on Tuesday. With us there is nobody else to hand you to.

      Do you guarantee results?

      No, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Nobody controls what your customers do, and a guaranteed return on paid social is either padded into the price or quietly defined so it cannot be missed. What we will commit to is the work: the testing volume, the reporting, and telling you early and plainly if the channel is not paying you back.

      Who owns the ad account and the creative?

      You do, always. We work inside your Business Manager under your assets, never ours. Every asset we produce is yours to keep and reuse after we stop working together, with no licence and no clawback.

      Do I need to be on TikTok too?

      Probably eventually, not necessarily now. We deliberately concentrate on Instagram and Facebook because that is where we are genuinely excellent, and a brand at your stage is usually better served by one channel run properly than three run adequately.

      What do you need from me?

      Access, product to shoot, and roughly an hour a week. The single biggest predictor of whether this works is how quickly you turn around creative approvals — the brands that grow fastest are the ones that reply the same day.

      What if it doesn't work?

      Then we tell you before you notice, we tell you why, and you leave on thirty days. We would rather lose the work than keep billing a founder for a channel that is not paying them back — the referral is worth more to us than the invoice.
